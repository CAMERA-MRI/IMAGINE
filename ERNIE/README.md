<p style="text-align: justify;">
We introduce the ESMRMB-IMAGINE- MRI Education (ERNIE) toolkit, designed to extend this hands-on collaborative model through a train-the-trainer approach. ERNIE is a frugal educational toolkit for on-site construction of a 50 mT OS-LF ‘mice-like’ brain MRI scanner that can be readily reproduced in any setting using common assembly tools and without prior MRI engineering experience. The toolkit was designed as a reusable teaching resource consists of:</p>

   1) a 30-minute lecture on OS-LF MRI concepts
   2) an animation of step-by-step guide of scanner and robot assembly and field mapping (quality assurance)
   3) 3D printing instructions and bill of materials for the magnet and robotic field mapping parts.
   4) tools for onsite assembly.
   5) Instructions and software to map the field’s homogeneity.
   6) The magnet is a 7-ring, 80mm Halbach array producing ~50 mT and made from 99 neodymium (N48) magnets.
<p style="text-align: justify;">
Rings and lids are 3D printed and digitally prototyped in Autodesk Inventor. A Genetic Algorithm was used for design optimization. The robot is a 3-axis Cartesian positioner with a GM2 Gaussmeter (Alpha Labs, DC Milligauss Meter Model MGM) as an end effector, capable of automatically mapping the B₀ field. It uses three stepper motors, TB6600 drivers, and an Arduino UNO R4 Minima. Each motor moves along a short linear stage. The toolkit also includes all tools, accessories, and software required for magnet and robot assembly for a team of learners.</p>
   
**Educational Sessions**
<p style="text-align: justify;">
The toolkit (ERNIE) was implemented in October 2025 at the European Society of Magnetic Resonance in Medicine and Biology (EMSMRB) CAMERA Pre-Congress workshop to pilot its use as a self-sustaining OS MRI education resource for settings that do not have well-equipped LF lab training spaces. A second  toolkit (ERNIE 1a) was developed to evaluate the kit’s reproducibility before creating the kit’s free online tutorial with the concepts lecture, animated assembly guides, and field mapping test software. ERNIE is currently being assembled, and its reproducibility and MRI engineering pedagogy are being evaluated by 200 students and researchers across 5 events from March to May 2026, including at The Neuro (Open Science), Polytechnique Montreal, The Douglas Mental Hospital, Montreal General Hospital, McGill Library, Montreal Public Library, and at the International Society of Magnetic Resonance in Medicine (ISMRM) conference in Cape Town, South Africa. </p>
